# Quest 5: A Hunter's Name

Borak names Grik a hunter of Sundrop. The family shares dinner, Old Mott tells
the old story until Borak stops him, Grik walks Lirra home, and the day ends.

Drogg came through the gate singing. The whole village heard him. Half of them followed him here.

The Boar King of Silverpine. You read the land, you fed the village, and you faced the king of beasts.

Kneel, Grik.

By the Three Trials, you are a hunter of Sundrop. Your name goes on the lodge wall, beside mine and my father's.

This knife was the first I ever carried. It is yours now. Keep it sharp, and never draw it for pride.

[Grik] Father, I...

Go home. Tikka has been cooking since noon, and if we are late, she will make us regret it.

Take a few coins too. Oren will want to see what a real hunter buys.

::: dialogue
npc: mott-hearth
branch: objective:hear-motts-tale
speaker: Old Mott
:::

Sit by the fire, boy. A hunter should know where he comes from.

Long ago, the world was at peace. Goblins, orcs, elves, even the demons of the north. We traded, we feasted, we married into each other's clans. A long peace, longer than anyone could count.

Then a hero came across the sea. A human, with a sword that burned like the sun.

He said the demons were a darkness on the world. And the humans who followed him believed him.

The demons fell. All of them. And when there were no demons left, the humans looked at the rest of us, and they saw beasts.

They called it the Great Hunt. Village after village burned. The ones who lived ran into the mountains and the deep woods.

[Borak] Father. Not tonight.

[Old Mott] He is a hunter now, Borak. He should know what hunts us.

[Borak] He will. Not tonight.

...As you say.

One more thing, boy. Under the shrine there is an old tunnel, sealed since before your father was born. No one goes in. No one. Do you understand?

[Grik] Yes, Grandfather.

Good. Now, your sister says Lirra is about to leave, and someone should walk her home.

[Grik] It's just an orc arrow.

Then it's a lucky orc arrow. Keep it anyway.

Tomorrow. The river. Don't oversleep this time.

Grik lies awake for a long time, turning the sun-stamped arrow over in his fingers.

Through the wall he can hear Drogg snoring and Tikka telling him to stop. Old Mott hums an old song by the fire.

Tomorrow, the river.

He sleeps.
